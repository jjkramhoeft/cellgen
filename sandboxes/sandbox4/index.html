Statistics

cellgen · Sandbox 4 · Terrain

An unbounded base map in metres, built from three levels of Voronoi cells. Every cell is a pure function of the seed and the parameters, so the world has no edge: pan anywhere and the same seed always gives the same map.

The three levels

  • Regions (128 km, straight borders) own the elevation zones whose site lies inside them. Each region picks 0–2 ocean seeds and 2–4 elevation zone merges among its elevation zones.
  • Elevation zones (32 km, warped borders) are the land/ocean unit. Each elevation zone is ocean, flat, lowland, hills or mountains (the map colours) and picks 2–4 patch merges among the patches it owns.
  • Patches (8 km, warped borders) carry detail: each has a biome; later also towns, ore, factions.

Oceans

  • Seeds: each region turns 0–2 of its elevation zones into ocean.
  • Links: with a 40 % chance a seed links to its nearest other seed, and with a 5 % double link chance to its two nearest seeds; every elevation zone on the straight line between them becomes ocean, so several seeds can join into one ocean.
  • Growth: every linked ocean elevation zone converts its 3 / 2 / 1 / 0 closest land neighbours with chances 10 / 33 / 33 / 23 %.
  • Colour by Ocean genesis and turn on Ocean seeds & links and Growth arrows to see each step.

Rivers

  • Grown per region by space colonization: a root at the point of every ocean elevation zone touching the region, and 10 attraction points in every inland (non-coastal) land patch.
  • Each step, nodes grow towards the attractors pulling on them, plus some random motion, until every attractor of the region is reached.
  • Line width = attractors reached upstream. Turn on Attractor points to see the attractors (unreached in red) and the roots (rings).
  • Lakes come after the rivers and always sit on one: each patch with a river gets a small chance of a lake (6 % by default, 1.5–5 km across), with a noise-shaped outline clipped to its region. No lakes in ocean or mountains zones, in deserts or in river-poor flat zones (left panel, Lakes; shown with the rivers). The inspector says whether a patch may hold a lake.

Terrain (Sandbox 4)

  • A height map fitted to the map, computed per region on a raster: river beds rise upstream by the stream-power law (S = S₀·flow−θ), hillslopes rise from the rivers and the coast with a steepness per elevation zone type (a geodesic distance), and ridges follow region borders and the divides between river systems.
  • Then detail noise (none on the river beds), the rivers are burnt in, and an ε-Priority-Flood fills every pit so all land drains to the sea or over a region border.
  • Colour by Height, or turn on Hillshade for any colour mode. Turn on 3D landscape (T) to see the current view in 3D: drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, Shift/right-drag to pan.
  • Regions are computed as you look (a few tenths of a second each); zoomed far out the terrain is skipped.

Initial climate

  • An initial temperature map (−10 to 30 °C by default) and an initial precipitation map (100 to 12 000 mm per year on a log scale, so the middle is about 1 100), each an fBm of Perlin noise with its own feature size, octaves, lacunarity, gain and range (left panel, Climate sections).
  • Turn on Temperature overlay or Precipitation overlay under Display to draw them 50 % transparent on top of the map and rivers; the inspector shows both values under the cursor.
  • A prosperity map (1 to 100 by default, unitless) built the same way, with its own seed and parameters (left panel, Prosperity). It sets how often gold, silver, gems and luxuries turn up among the resources, and it is one of the five factors of the city and town locations; turn on Prosperity overlay under Display to see it (dark purple = poor, gold = prosperous).
  • A supernatural map (0 to 100 by default, unitless): how strong magic is, for magic resources and other fantasy game play. The same Perlin fBm with its own seed and much finer features (10 km, 3 octaves by default; left panel, Supernatural). It does not affect anything yet; turn on Supernatural overlay under Display to see it (near black = mundane, violet → glowing cyan = strong magic).

Biomes

  • Every land patch gets a biome from the initial climate at its site. The temperature is lowered by a cooling curve over the patch’s mean height from the terrain: a smooth curve through 5 points you set (height → °C cooler, 6.5 °C per km by default), plotted in the Biomes section; until its terrain is computed a typical height for the elevation zone type stands in.
  • River-poor flat elevation zones are drier (precipitation × 0.2), so they favour desert.
  • The aridity P / (20·T + 140) and a thermal belt pick one of 15 biomes, from ice, tundra and taiga over temperate forests and grassland to hot desert, savanna and rainforest (left panel, Biomes).
  • Mangrove: a coastal patch (bordering the ocean) in a flat or lowland zone becomes mangrove where it is at least 20 °C and not desert (aridity ≥ 0.5); both are settings. Never inland or in hills or mountains.
  • Colour by Biomes (2) to see them; the inspector shows each patch’s biome and the values that picked it.

Resources

  • Every patch gets an amount (0–1) for each of 24 raw resources in seven groups (food, materials, fuel, salt, metals, luxuries, animals), and the variant that fits it best (“grain: barley”, “timber: teak”, “iron ore: bog iron”).
  • Potentials follow the climate and the water: crops by growing-season temperature and rainfall (a river irrigates dry land), pasture, game and timber by biome, fish by coast, river and lake. Spices, stimulants and dyes grow only in some elevation zones.
  • Deposits (stone, crystal, salt, ores, gems) are rare hashed finds. Each land elevation zone has a bedrock class that decides what it can hold; copper and tin never share a zone, salt is scarce, and rivers carry gold and tin down from the hills as placers.
  • Prosperity sets how often the valuable ones turn up: gold, silver & lead, gems & pearls, spices, stimulants, dyes, furs and ivory. A quarter as often where the prosperity map is at a quarter of its range, 2.25 times as often at three quarters.
  • The inspector lists the resources of the patch under the cursor; the city and town locations are scored on them.

City & town locations

  • Every region gets about 20 potential locations for cities (◆) and towns (●). Each land patch is scored on five factors: resources (read from the resource layer: food of its catchment, fresh water, timber / stone / clay and fuel, fish, and trade goods such as salt, ores and luxuries), geography (buildable ground, a waterside site, defence, depth inside the region), trade & travel (river mouth, harbour, navigable river, confluence, or a central place), climate and prosperity (the prosperity map).
  • The viability score (0–100) is the weighted mean of the five, scaled down to 0 where there is no fresh water or the temperature is unlivable. The best-scoring patches, kept apart by a spacing, become the locations; the top ones, far apart and well inside the region, are cities (left panel, City & town locations).
  • Display → City & town locations (C) shows them, coloured by viability; the Location viability slider hides the ones below a score. Zoom in for the scores, and hover a location (or any land patch) to see its factors in the inspector.

Travel network

  • Display → Travel network (P) connects the cities and towns that are on show (the Location viability slider; zoomed far out only the cities): roads over land, river routes along navigable rivers and sea lanes between harbours and river mouths.
  • Roads cost more through hills and far more through mountains, deserts and ice, on steep ground, at height, across lakes and big rivers; boats are cheap per kilometre but pay for loading at both ends, so short hops go by road.
  • Neighbouring places are linked by their cheapest route; a link is left out when a short detour over cheaper links does the same job, so everything stays connected without a line between every two places. Links between two cities are drawn heavier.
  • Travel routes switches between straight lines from place to place and routes that follow the terrain: roads wind along valleys and over the lowest passes, river routes follow the river, sea lanes go round the land.

Navigation

  • Scroll to zoom at the cursor, drag to pan, pinch on touch, double-click zooms in (Shift zooms out).
  • Click pins the elevation zone (yellow outline) and patch (cyan outline) under the cursor; Esc releases.
  • Shift+drag or M measures a distance, with travel times for a horse, a marching legion and a hiker.
